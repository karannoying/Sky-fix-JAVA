# Verification ledger

CLAUDE.md rule 5: *no invented facts. Unverified claims carry a literal `verify:` marker.*

`SourceRulesTest` already proves the markers exist. It could not prove they were **actionable** —
a marker saying "check this" leaves the next person to rediscover which document, which table, and
what the number should come out as. This file closes that gap: every marker still standing in
`src/main` or `data/` has an entry here giving the exact source, the exact check, and what to do
with the result. `MarkerLedgerTest` fails the build if a marker exists without one.

A marker is not a defect. It is the honest label on a number that has a source which has not been
opened. Several of the numbers below are almost certainly right; the point is that "almost
certainly" is not the standard this project claims to hold itself to.

**Every item below is blocked on a document that the build environment cannot reach.** The
environment resolves no external host — `ntrs.nasa.gov`, `weather.uwyo.edu`, `iupac.org`,
`ciaaw.org` and `doi.org` all return no response at all. None of them is blocked on more work in
this repository, and none can be cleared by measurement against DS-6, because DS-6 is generated by
the very models the markers are about.

## Still open

### V-1 · Lifting-gas molar masses
**File** `src/main/java/com/skyfix/domain/LiftGas.java`
**Claim** He = 4.002602e-3 kg/mol, H₂ = 2.01588e-3 kg/mol.
**Source** IUPAC/CIAAW standard atomic weights — `ciaaw.org/atomic-weights.htm`.
**Check** He is monatomic, so its molar mass is the standard atomic weight directly. H₂ is
diatomic: take twice the standard atomic weight of hydrogen. Both are quoted as intervals in the
current tables; record the conventional value and its uncertainty.
**If it differs** These enter the model only through gas density, so a fourth-digit revision moves
a landing point by metres. Update the constants and cite the table in §17 of the report.

### V-2 · DS-1 sounding layout and archive terms
**File** `src/main/java/com/skyfix/io/WyomingSoundingReader.java`
**Claim** The fixed-width column layout documented in that class's Javadoc is the archive's.
**Source** University of Wyoming upper-air archive — `weather.uwyo.edu/upperair/sounding.html`.
**Check** Fetch any station and observation time in the TEXT:LIST format and compare the header
and column positions against the Javadoc. Then read the site's terms before redistributing a real
file in this repository.
**If it differs** The parser reads by column position with a whitespace-split fallback, so small
width differences do not break it; a different column *order* would. `SoundingReaderTest` asserts
against the committed sample, so update both together.

### V-3 · DS-1 ships a synthetic sounding
**File** `data/soundings/SYNTHETIC_2026-09-14_00Z.txt` — carries `[PLACEHOLDER — DS-1]`.
**Claim** None. The file says in its own header that it is generated, and every consumer of it
says so too. This entry exists because the blueprint asks for three *real* cached soundings.
**Source** As V-2.
**Check** Download three real soundings covering the mission region, confirm the licence permits
redistribution, and commit them beside the synthetic one rather than in place of it — the
synthetic profile is what several tests are written against.
**If it differs** A real sounding veers considerably more with altitude than the synthetic one,
which is exactly why §11 of the report says the 150:1 footprint aspect ratio is a property of the
shipped profile and not a general result. A real profile would make that section's argument
stronger, not weaker, but the numbers in it would change.

### V-4 · Pre-flight dispersion spreads
**File** `src/main/java/com/skyfix/domain/DispersionSpec.java`
**Claim** The default pre-flight spreads describe how well a team knows its balloon before launch.
**Source** A manufacturer's stated tolerance, or a population of real flights with recorded fill
data. **No synthetic dataset can supply this** — DS-6's truth parameters are drawn from this very
specification, so fitting the spreads to DS-6 would measure this constructor and nothing else. An
earlier note in the file proposed exactly that and has been withdrawn as circular.
**Check** Compare against a manufacturer's quoted tolerance on burst diameter and against whatever
fill-mass repeatability the launch team actually achieves.
**If it differs** Everything in the footprint scales with these. They are the single largest
unmeasured input to O2.

### V-5 · Barometric altitude sigma factor
**File** `src/main/java/com/skyfix/estimation/GaussianMeasurementModel.java`
**Claim** `PRESSURE_ALTITUDE_SIGMA_FACTOR = 4.0`.
**Measured, partly.** Over 158,753 DS-6 samples the pressure altitude differs from the GPS
altitude with mean −0.01 m and sd 17.12 m, giving a factor of **1.71** against the 10 m GPS sigma.
That figure is a floor and is not the answer: DS-6 generates its pressures from the same USSA-1976
model the reader inverts, so the round trip contains the barometer's noise and none of the model
bias the factor mainly exists for. The mean of −0.01 m is the giveaway.
**Source** A real flight log carrying both GPS and barometric altitude, flown on a day whose
profile departs from the standard atmosphere.
**Check** Compute the same difference over that log, per altitude band. Expect a systematic offset
in the troposphere that DS-6 structurally cannot show.
**If it differs** 4.0 is the conservative direction — it weights the barometric channel more
loosely than DS-6 requires, costing a little information on dropout samples but keeping the filter
from chasing an atmospheric offset. A measured factor should replace it in either direction.

### V-6 · DS-3 reference table transcription
**File** `data/reference/ussa1976.csv`
**Claim** 25 altitudes of temperature, pressure and density for USSA-1976.
**Corroborated, not transcribed.** Two independent third-party implementations agree to 0.00987%
(density at 47 km), ten times inside T-V1's 0.1% tolerance, so they corroborate each other as an
oracle independent of SKYFIX. That is a real argument and it is not a primary source.
**Source** NOAA/NASA/USAF, *U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976*, NOAA-S/T 76-1562, Table I.
**Check** Re-transcribe the 25 rows by hand from Table I and replace the file header with that
citation. `./scripts/run.sh validate` then re-runs T-V1 against the transcribed values.
**If it differs** T-V1 is the gate on FR-2.1 and it currently passes at 0.1% relative. A
transcription error in either direction shows up there immediately.

### V-7 · DS-4 catalogue balloon figures
**File** `data/missions/balloon.json`
**Claim** `burst_diameter_m: 7.0` and `launch_diameter_m: 1.8` for a 1200 g latex sounding balloon.
**Source** The manufacturer's datasheet (Totex or Kaymont) for the 1200 g balloon.
**Check** Read burst diameter and nominal launch diameter off the datasheet for the actual balloon
the mission will fly, and record which manufacturer and which product code in the `_comment`.
**If it differs** Burst diameter sets burst altitude, which sets everything downstream. The
estimator recovers a *burst scale* against this nominal, so a wrong nominal does not break the
filter — it relabels what the scale means, which matters for the report but not for the footprint.

### V-8 · EGM96 geoid undulation
**File** `data/missions/mission.json`
**Claim** The launch site's ground elevation is applied as a single constant, and the geoid
undulation folded into it is unconfirmed.
**Source** An EGM96 undulation value for the launch region.
**Check** Look up the undulation at the launch coordinates and confirm whether the elevation in
`mission.json` is above the ellipsoid or above mean sea level. CLAUDE.md rule 3 says altitudes are
geometric above MSL, so the constant must be MSL.
**If it differs** A constant offset moves every altitude in the flight by the same amount. In
India the undulation is tens of metres, which is small against a 30 km ascent but not against the
ground-elevation constant it is being folded into.

These are open for reasons other than an unreachable document, and are listed so the grep and this
file agree.

| DGCA/AAI notification rules for unmanned free balloons in India | `docs/BLUEPRINT.md` §5 | Persona P3 is served qualitatively; no regulatory number is quoted anywhere, so nothing depends on this. Out of scope rather than unverified. |
| Sphere drag-crisis Reynolds range | `docs/BLUEPRINT.md` §10 | Used only to argue that a constant Cd is defensible over the balloon's Re range. Cd is *estimated* from telemetry, so the argument is a justification for the model choice, not an input to it. |
| Liu & West (1999); Gordon, Salmond & Smith (1993) | `docs/report/report.md` §17 | Full citations are known but not checkable offline, and rule 5 forbids writing a DOI that has not been opened. Ten minutes with a library catalogue clears both. |

The measurement is complete and says the criterion is unachievable for an identifiability reason (§11 of the report). Amending a graded acceptance criterion is the course owner's call, not the implementer's. |

## Cleared by measurement

Kept so the difference between "unverifiable here" and "nobody measured it" stays visible.

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| BLUEPRINT §10: "LHS reaches a stable 95% ellipse in ~400 members where plain MC needs ~1,500" | **Measured and retired at ~2.2x, not 3.75x.** Seven independent seeds per point across six member counts; LHS's seed-to-seed scatter is lower at every count. `SamplerConvergenceTest` re-derives it in about three minutes; ADR-14 carries the table. A first attempt used one seed per point, which is a single sample of the very spread in question, and reported the opposite. |
| `DispersionSpec`: "replace these spreads with ones fitted to the DS-6 posterior" | **Withdrawn as circular.** DS-6's truths are drawn from this spec. Now V-4. |
| `GaussianMeasurementModel`: "replace 4.0 with a figure fitted from DS-6" | **Partly measured** at 1.71, and shown to be a floor rather than an answer. Now V-5. |
